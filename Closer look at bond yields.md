# Yields

There are a lot of assumptions built into the sigle number YTM and it's not immediately obvious
what my return from a bond would be just from looking at the YTM.

## Yields are market determined

When people say "rates changed", it's the market rates ( in general ) which will affect the prices
of bonds so that their NEW YTMs for a **new buyer** would reflect that new rate regime.

So if I bought at a certain yield ( which is no longer available in market, rates decreased ),
will I earn that yield as my return?

No, as YTM assumes that you MUST reinvest coupons at YTM as well, which is no longer possible.
But assume for a moment that it is.

As the market rates fall, suddenly my bond is attractive to a new buyer, price will rise to ensure
it still offers market yield to the buyer ( assuming no liquidity issues ).

Do I gain anything from the price gain?
Only if I sell, otherwise a pull to par will make any intermediate MTM changes irrelevant.
Note that you can conversely make a huge MTM loss as well.

Ok, assuming that the rates do not change and I can re-invest at YTM, do I still earn YTM
if I sell before maturity? What about roll down yield?
