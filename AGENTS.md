This repository contains my finance learning notes. Most tasks involve clarifying concepts, checking formulas, and fixing LaTeX.

- Use dollar-based KaTeX syntax: `$...$` for inline math and `$$...$$` for display math, as appropriate.
- Use plain `$E[X]$` for expectation, not `$\mathbb{E}[X]$`.
- Write equations concisely. Keep each equation or chain of equalities on one line; do not split at equals signs.
- Keep the focus on the finance. Define terms and symbols, but keep explanations brief and avoid long paragraphs.
- Follow [Conventions.md](Conventions.md) for notation. State assumptions that affect a formula, including compounding, time units, and currency quote direction.
- Distinguish exact identities, approximations, and model assumptions. Preserve the reasoning and useful examples when editing.
- Keep writing plain and concise. Preserve the author's voice, remove filler, and avoid inflated claims or invented facts.
- Use familiar words and simple verbs. Keep necessary finance terms, define unfamiliar acronyms, and use terminology consistently.
- Prefer active voice when it reads naturally. Remove repeated explanations and needless adverbs; preserve useful intuition and reasoning.
- State claims directly. Avoid staged openings, rhetorical questions, slogans, forced groups of three, and formulaic contrasts. Keep contrasts that explain a financial distinction.
- Keep uncertainty that affects a claim. Remove stacked hedges, but never fill gaps with plausible guesses or invented citations.
- Write notes so they stand alone. Leave out chat history, editing commentary, greetings, praise, and generic closing summaries.
- Use American English in prose; preserve quotations, identifiers, and established finance terminology.
- Use sentence case for headings and keep their hierarchy shallow. Use lists for parallel items or steps, and use bold sparingly.
- Keep each prose paragraph on one source line. Before finishing, check that edits preserve the finance and remove repetition without losing information.
